Appalachian Trail Thru-Hike

In 2019, I walked from Georgia to Maine—2,190 miles over six months. The Appalachian Trail is more than a hike; it's a complete reset of perspective. You learn what you actually need (not much), how to be comfortable with discomfort, and what happens when you remove all the noise from daily life.

Key Lessons

  • Simplicity works. You can live out of a 30-pound backpack. Most of what we own is noise.
  • Small steps compound. 2,190 miles is just 15 miles a day, 165 times. Same applies to any big goal.
  • Weather is happening. You can't control conditions, only your response. Plan for the plan to change.
  • Community appears. "Trail magic" happens when you least expect it. People are generally good.

Geography & Maps

I'm slightly obsessed with maps. GeoGuessr is my meditation app. Google Earth is my screensaver. I find genuine joy in understanding how landscapes, borders, and cities came to be where they are.

This isn't just a quirky hobby—it shapes how I think about products. Geography is about understanding systems: why do people cluster here, move there, build this way? Those same questions apply to user behavior and product design.

GeoGuessr

Top 1% globally in competitive play. There's something deeply satisfying about identifying a location from a single street view image—reading clues from road signs, vegetation, architectural styles, and sun position.

Maps Collection

I collect vintage maps, particularly topographic surveys and transit maps. There's history in how we choose to represent space—what we include, what we leave out, how we orient ourselves.

Craft Beer & Cicerone Certification

I'm a Certified Cicerone—think sommelier, but for beer. What started as "I like IPAs" became a deep dive into brewing science, beer history, and flavor analysis.

The certification process taught me that expertise requires both breadth and depth. You need to understand history, science, and sensory evaluation. You need to taste systematically, not just casually. Same principles apply to product management: know the market, know the mechanics, develop a framework for evaluation.
